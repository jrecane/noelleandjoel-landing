  • How We Met

    Joel's Side

    Noëlle and I first met in 2021, when our shared love of adventure, the outdoors, and a mutual inability to hold down a full-time job led us both to hike the Pacific Crest Trail. We crossed paths around 600 miles in, just before the Sierra Nevada — and ended up hiking the rest of the trail together. Well, almost: somewhere in the Sierras, Noëlle decided to sleep in one morning, never quite caught back up, and left me to hike solo for the next two weeks.

    What started as a close friendship and a surprisingly productive hiking partnership eventually turned into love — though you'd never guess it from the first photo I ever took of her.

    It would take a little while longer before I started taking pictures that actually included her face.

    A few more from that summer on the trail:

    After reaching the Canadian border, we both found our way back to Boston — and somewhere in all that overlap, the friendship turned into something more. Not ones to learn our lesson, we went on to walk across the country together again, and eventually traded the trail for Alaska for good.

    By the fall of 2023, we were homeless and unemployed yet again — but this time, we knew Boston wasn't where we wanted to land. As luck would have it, my brother Robin was about to deploy and needed someone to watch his house (and his dog) in Alaska. So we made the move: a return to Alaska for me, and a bold leap for Noëlle, who'd never actually been to Alaska before (Sitka doesn't count). But just as significant as the move itself was the time we got to spend with my brother Robin's dog, the dearly departed Saul, King of Dogs.

    Since then, life has been one long string of joy. We've had adventures, built a life together, made friends, and bought a home. Noëlle works at the Anchorage Museum and has probably surpassed me in Alaska knowledge and trivia by now. She's run a marathon, become principal bassist for the Anchorage Symphony, learned to backcountry ski, and turned into a highly competent — and braver than me — whitewater paddler. She's caught and killed her first salmon (she only cried a little), seen the northern lights, and had her fair share of run-ins with brown bears and moose, among so much more.

    Noëlle's Side

    Joel and I met by complete happenstance on the Pacific Crest Trail in June of 2021. I had started the trail about a week before we met and had mostly been hiking alone until I met a group of hikers at some “trail magic” (this was not really trail magic because some guy named Ron was selling us drinks, NOT giving them to us in the spirit of trail magic, but I digress) between Tehachapi and Kennedy Meadows South. This group of hikers invited me to hike with them to Kennedy Meadows. After spending a day off eating burgers, drinking beer, and being disgusting hiker trash, I decided to hike out with Joel and the rest of the group.

    From that day on, Joel and I developed a close friendship and hiked most of the remaining 1500 miles of trail together. I immediately admired his grounding energy, resilience, and sense of humor. I learned that he had been in the Air Force, and before the PCT had been living in Alaska. He listened to me talk about bass and being a musician. We discovered that we grew up less than 30 miles apart in Massachusetts.

    We ate lunch together almost every day, cowboy camped under the stars, hitchhiked in the back of strangers’ trucks, shared scummy motel rooms with our friends, destroyed our feet, lost unhealthy amounts of weight, snacked on shitty cake frosting on bagels, smoked “downhill” cigarettes, binge ate at town stops, met shady people, met cool people, and ultimately finished the trail. We experienced the highs, lows, freedom, and the privilege of embarking on such a grand adventure together.

    After the PCT, Joel and I both moved back to Boston. Our friendship eventually turned into something more when we kissed at Model Cafe in Brighton. We both felt Boston was not our final destination. We decided we both wanted to move “somewhere else”, but first, we would hike the Continental Divide trail. The CDT, with all of its challenges, brought us even closer together. At some point on our trip we decided to move to Alaska where we would house sit for Joel’s brother, Robin, and take care of his dog, Saul.

    Since moving to Alaska, we have built a beautiful life filled with love, friends, and adventure. We are so excited to get married at one of our favorite places, Arctic Valley.

    Joel became my adventure partner on the PCT, but little did I know he would become my partner in everything for life.
